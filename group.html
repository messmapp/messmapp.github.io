Research Group

Multi-messenger astrophysics, high-energy time-domain astrophysics

Group Focus

We study multi-messenger and time-domain astrophysics, with a particular focus on high-energy gamma-ray transients and variable sources. Our research aims to understand the physical processes driving activity in active galactic nuclei (AGN) and to investigate the origin of astrophysical neutrinos. To address these questions, our group combines state-of-the-art theoretical models with broad multiwavelength observations of AGN and data from major multimessenger facilities, including the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the ANTARES/KM3NeT Neutrino Telescopes.

The group is based at the University of Würzburg, at the Chair for Astronomy, and at DESY in Zeuthen (Germany). It is primarily supported by the ERC Starting Grant awarded to Prof. Buson in 2020 for the project *MessMapp*. Several additional research projects are also ongoing. The project *HadroPrint* (PI: Prof. Buson), starting in 2027 with support from the Deutsches Zentrum für Luft- und Raumfahrt (DLR, German Aerospace Center), aims to investigate the X-ray properties of AGN in connection with neutrinos. In addition, in 2027, the new project *Jet Physics Across the Multimessenger Spectrum* (PI: Prof. Buson), supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) within the DFG Research Unit Relativistic Jets, will investigate the real-time domain and neutrino astrophysics. The team is actively involved in several NASA missions and collaborations, including the Fermi-LAT Collaboration and the forthcoming COSI gamma-ray space telescope. We are also active members of the KM3NeT Collaboration.

    and DESY, Zeuthen

    I am an astrophysicist working in multi-messenger astrophysics with strong focus on high-energy and astro-particle physics. I have been the convener of the Active Galactic Nuclei working group and time-doamin group of the Fermi-LAT Collaboration. Since 2018 I am a junior Professor at the Institut für Theoretische Physik und Astrophysik at the Julius-Maximilians Universitat Würzburg (DE).

    PostDoc

    DESY, Zeuthen

    I completed my PhD at the CPPM of Marseille working on low-energy supernova neutrino detection with the KM3NeT neutrino telescope. Then I moved to the Ruhr-University Bochum, working on high energy neutrino searches from optical transients with the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, and on the development of the IceCube realtime infrastructure. As part of the Messmapp group, I investigate the the blazar-neutrino connection.

    PostDoc

    DESY, Zeuthen

    After focusing on radio galaxies during my PhD, I am now investigating their more powerful cousins, the blazars, with the aim of understanding a bit more what processes take place in their core and how we can explain how we observe them.
